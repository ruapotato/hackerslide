# Pre-fabrication review, 2026-11-25

Before any Gerbers leave the repository, the routed boards were checked in seven ways. Three checks were done by
independent reviewers (AI agents that had not seen the author's reasoning, working only from the repository and
the datasheets); the rest by the author with scripts that are now in `hardware/scripts`. No review by a person is
planned (David, 2026-11-25): the boards are to be built and tested, so everything that could be settled without
parts was settled here, and everything that could not has a bench step and a fallback. `hardware/scripts/verify.sh`
repeats all the automatic checks.

## What was checked

| # | Check | How | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Footprints against manufacturer drawings | Independent reviewer, pad by pad | One near-blocker, fixed; see below |
| 2 | Schematic against datasheets | Independent reviewer, every IC and connector | No pin errors; three major design findings, fixed or decided |
| 3 | Stack-up and pair geometry | `impedance.py`, a 2-D field solution | DSI gap changed to 0.15 mm (100 ohm); USB unchanged (89 ohm) |
| 4 | Power paths | `power_paths.py`: narrowest point of each copper area | Two necks of 0.6 mm found and widened |
| 5 | Mechanical fit | Enclosure model with the module's real heights and the battery connector; `fit_check.py` for every part's height at its position; LuckFox's 3D model laid over the sockets and spacers | Battery plug had no room: connector turned, body 2 mm longer. Module fits the sockets within 0.04 mm |
| 6 | Assembly and stock | BOM regenerated against the JLCPCB parts API | See `bom/` |
| 7 | Fabrication files | `fab_outputs.sh`, then `gerber_check.py`: every copper layer drawn by an independent Gerber reader and laid over the board file; drill files against the board's holes; mask and paste against the pads | All four boards: copper identical, every hole accounted for, no pad without mask opening or paste |
| 9 | Crosstalk of auto-routed signals | `coupling.py`: side-by-side run lengths per net pair | Worst pair well under 1 %: [carrier-board.md](carrier-board.md) |
| 10 | Land patterns the first reviewer could not get drawings for | Second independent reviewer, from archived manufacturer drawings | Molex 5268, JST SH, MAX17048, both Wuerth spacers: all match. [footprint-review.md](footprint-review.md) |
| 8 | Open physical questions | Independent researcher, public documents only | Four of five answered; see [open-questions.md](open-questions.md) |

## Findings and what was done

| Finding | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|
| FH34SRJ hold-down lands 0.8 mm out of place on all four cable connectors (a 3.3 mm dimension read centre to centre; it is edge to edge) | Would have cost a board spin | Fixed in `mklib.fh34`; both boards regenerated |
| Charge current limited only by software: up to about 1.8 A into a 1.4 A pack with the charger's reset values | Major | R12 300 -> 470 ohm: 1.25 A at most, with no software |
| Power-button Schottkys (1 A parts) leak enough to hold the charger's /QON low when 3.3 V is off | Major | BAT54WS, same footprint |
| No reliable way to power the Core3566 back on after `poweroff` | Major | Unfitted long-press power-cut network on the fuse's enable pin (C90, R91, D9); pin 99 stays an open question |
| Discharge limiter set above its rated continuous current | Minor | Replaced by a TPS259571 eFuse at 3.0 A (David) |
| VBUS TVS clamps above the charger's 22 V limit | Minor | SMF6.0A |
| HP_DET can be driven below ground when playing with no plug | Note | 10k in series with the codec pin (R69) |
| USB data pair carried a 45 mm stub to the unfitted adapter-detection links | Found while routing | Links moved to the connector |
| VBUS inner-layer area necked to 0.6 mm at the USB connector and at the DSI clock vias | Found by `power_paths.py` | Area widened; now 1.5 mm at its narrowest |
| Battery connector pointed into the battery bay; no room for the plug or the leads | Found when the connector was added to the enclosure model | Header turned to face the module side, backlight parts moved to make room, body 72 -> 74 mm |
| Module height in the model was the CM4's (5.08 mm); LuckFox's own 3D model gives 4.50 mm, SoC lid at 3.6 mm | Model error | `config.py` corrected; the thermal gap is 1.9 mm, not 0.42: [thermal.md](thermal.md) |
| PJ-342 pad 4 is 2.6 mm long on the drawing, 2.0 mm in the footprint | Minor | Fixed |
| TPS61230 on the older DRC land pattern (0.15 mm oversize exposed pad) | Minor | Left: solderable |
| Keyboard diodes can be 0.55 mm high at maximum against a 0.5 mm limit under the keycap flange | Minor | Closed: the limit was stated too tightly. The flange is 0.9 mm above the board and 0.77 mm when pressed |
| Wuerth M2.5 spacer has a 1.4 mm boss, so it shows 0.4 mm on the display side of the 1.0 mm carrier (the schematic said nothing protrudes) | Note | Note corrected; 1.6 mm is free there |
| GPIO_VREF parts were scattered by the placer and the router left them unjoined in one run | Found while routing | R30, C30, C37 hand-placed in a column |
| The touch connector J5 was fenced in by fuse-output copper on the inner layer and the display side; its signals could only leave across the backlight parts | Found while routing (several runs ended with 3-9 open) | 1.8 mm gap in the display-side strap below J5, the five signals laid by hand through it, pull-ups R40/R41 moved to where they come out |
| The backlight driver was the one switcher still placed automatically: output capacitor 5 mm from the diode, supply and switch pins crossed | Found while routing | Laid by hand like the charger and the boost (`carrier_cells.backlight`). Its switch node is 3.9 mm of track, 4.9 mm from the nearest DSI copper |
| The two pads of each push-button terminal were left for the router to join | Found while routing | Scripted links round the locating hole; R37 placed at the reset switch |
| The finishing loop could end on a worse board than one it had already reached, and its clean-up pass could open connections on an unfinished board | Tool fault | It keeps the best round; the clean-up runs only on a complete board. The router's hash seed is fixed so a rebuild repeats |
| PJ-342 may be a 3-pole jack (one reviewer, from the outline drawing); its circuit diagram shows four poles (the other) | Unsettled | Sample and multimeter |

## Independent layout review of the routed carrier (2026-11-26)

A reviewer that had seen none of the author's reasoning went through the routed board against the TI layout
sections. What it found and what was done:

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| About 200 auto-routed track ends touched their pad, via or neighbour only at the rim (3-40 um of overlap): invisible to DRC, a random open on a real board | Fixed on both boards: every such end is extended into the centre of what it joins (`gr_io.py anchor`) |
| HPGND joined ground 18 mm from the jack, not at it; two of the jack's ESD diodes reached ground through a 0.1 mm track | R49 moved to the jack with its own via; ground vias beside the diodes |
| Codec decoupling auto-placed 6-13 mm from the pins | Moved to the display side behind the codec, 0.5-5.6 mm. Hand layout of the codec is left for a second revision |
| +3V3 and +1V8 distributed on signal-width tracks | Left: see "Known compromises" in [carrier-board.md](carrier-board.md) |
| The upper display-side VSYS_LIM strap had no vias at its lower end, so it was not in parallel with In2 as the notes claimed; three vias carried the whole boost input | Three vias at each end of both straps; six at the boost inductor; three in the fuse's exposed pad |
| Backlight: a signal routed under the inductor and round the switch pad; one ground via | No-track area under the inductor, second ground via. LED_A clearance measured: 0.18 mm |
| Charger PMID capacitor's ground 3 mm from a via | Via on the ground bar 1 mm from C11 |
| Switcher loops return through the plane, boost input capacitor 4.4 mm from its pin, narrow SW/VOUT tracks | Left: see "Known compromises" |

Checked and found fine by the reviewer: In1 unbroken (slots under 4 mm, none under a pair), USB and DSI pairs over
solid ground, fuse pinout, gauge sense point, VBAT, VBUS and +5V copper.

## Verified correct

Pin numbers and functions of every IC for the package used; the DF40 sockets against the Raspberry Pi CM4IO board
(numbering, row order, no mirroring); the panel and touch pinouts against the Focus LCDs specification; the
battery connector's pin order against the pack and Molex drawings (red on pin 1: the pack drawing and the Molex 5264 catalogue show the same view of the plug, with red at circuit 1; still measure
before plugging in); the boost, backlight and thermistor arithmetic; I2C addresses; all IC land patterns that had
a drawing.

## Made for the bench (2026-11-25)

Since the first boards are the review: pads on the debug UART beside the JST socket, pads on the system I2C bus, a
lamp on the 5 V rail, a test-point map with expected readings ([test-points.md](test-points.md)), a bring-up
order that powers one block at a time ([bring-up-plan.md](bring-up-plan.md)), and the kernel's requirements in one

## Not verified

See the last table of [open-questions.md](open-questions.md): each item there has a bench step and a fallback.
The largest are the battery plug's polarity (meter first, always), the Core3566's behaviour on pin 99 and the I/O
voltage of five of its pins, which no public document settles.
